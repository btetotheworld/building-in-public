# Disagreement, Corrections, and Course Changes

Public work attracts different interpretations. That is useful when disagreement is handled as information rather than as a contest.

## When feedback conflicts

1. Read the full comment before responding.
2. Separate the person's observation from your interpretation of it.
3. Ask a focused question if the concern is unclear.
4. Check the evidence and the project's stated goal.
5. Record the decision and what would make you revisit it.

## When the project changes direction

Do not quietly rewrite the record. Add a short note with:

- what the previous approach was;
- what evidence made it less useful;
- what the new approach is; and
- what remains unknown.

That keeps the history useful to people who arrive later.

## A response template

```markdown
Thanks for raising this. I understand the concern as:

> [Restate the feedback]

The current evidence suggests [what we know]. I will [next action].
I will revisit this if [clear trigger].
```

## Keep the record clean

Do not delete uncomfortable results just because the plan changed. Correct factual errors, protect private information, and label superseded decisions clearly.
